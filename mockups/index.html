Field Annotator v2 — three layouts

All three are live: tap the pitch to lay touches 1–6, tap a pin to turn it into a turnover, tap the try band at the attacking end to score, and arm a penalty then place it (or tap the armed button twice to drop it at the last ball position). The pitch is attack-normalised — whoever has the ball always attacks the same way, so "forward" never moves.

A · Full-bleed pitchBUILT

The pitch is the screen. Score strip on top, everything else floats over the turf.

  • Biggest tap target by a wide margin — the thing you hit most is the thing that's largest.
  • Penalties pinned under the try band; controls on the right edge, out of the tap path.
  • Last four as a scrollable chip strip along the bottom.

B · Split deck

Pitch on top, a fixed button deck below. Closest in feel to the v1 annotator.

  • Most legible: nothing overlaps the pitch, every control is labelled.
  • Costs roughly half the screen, so pitch taps are less precise.
  • Best if you want v2 to feel like v1 with a pitch bolted on.

C · Landscape two-thumb

Phone held sideways. Attack runs left→right, penalties under the left thumb, last-four down the right.

  • The pitch's real 70×50 shape fits landscape far better than portrait.
  • Both thumbs work; nothing overlays the playing area.
  • Awkward one-handed, and a landscape-only tool is a worse PWA.
Open full screen →

Recommendation: A. Tagging live is a speed-and-accuracy problem, and A spends the whole screen on the one control that needs both. B's deck is more readable but you read it once and then never look at it again — while the pitch is what your eye is on for eighty minutes. C's geometry is genuinely better, but a landscape-locked page is a worse home-screen app and worse one-handed on a sideline. A borrows C's best idea (the attack-normalised pitch) without the orientation lock.
